Accessibility Statement — GreenlitX

Last updated: [Date to be inserted on adoption, after legal review]

1. Our commitment

GreenlitX wants the platform to be usable by everyone, including people who use assistive technology.

Accessibility is an ongoing build standard for GreenlitX, not a one-time claim. This statement describes what GreenlitX aims to build in, what is known to be limited, and how to ask for help or an alternative way to access content.

2. Target standard

GreenlitX aims to design and test the platform against WCAG 2.1 Level AA as its working target. [Target standard and any audit plan to be confirmed by owner with legal review.]

GreenlitX has not had an independent accessibility audit or WCAG conformance certification, and this statement does not claim one. This statement must not be read as a guarantee that every page conforms to the target standard at all times.

3. What we build in

GreenlitX's build standards include:

  • Semantic page structure with headings and landmarks;
  • Labeled form fields and form error information that does not rely on color alone;
  • Visible keyboard focus states and keyboard-operable controls;
  • Color contrast checked for body text against the platform's dark theme, with bright accent colors reserved for highlights rather than relied on for small essential text;
  • Support for reduced-motion preferences — animated showcase and effects content stops or simplifies when a visitor's system requests reduced motion;
  • Responsive layouts that work at phone sizes and at zoom, with touch targets sized for phones, where most of the audience watches; and
  • Text alternatives or labels for meaningful images and controls, where the content is GreenlitX's own.

These are design and build practices. An individual page or a newly added feature may still contain a barrier — Section 5 explains how to report one.

4. Known limitations

Third-party players and films

Video playback uses established third-party players (for example, the Internet Archive and YouTube). Captions, audio description, transcripts, and player accessibility features depend on what the provider and the film's source copy offer.

GreenlitX cannot add captions or audio description to a film it does not host. Many older and public-domain titles predate captioning, and their source copies may have no captions available.

Project and concept materials

Posters and concept images are visual by nature. GreenlitX aims to provide a text description of a project's story and status in the project presentation alongside its artwork, so the essential information about a project does not exist only inside an image.

Availability of third-party content

A Watch Free film may be removed or restricted by its source provider. If an accessible version of the same film is lawfully available from the source, GreenlitX will consider linking or embedding that version on request — GreenlitX cannot create or host an accessible copy it has no right to make.

5. Feedback and alternative access

If something on GreenlitX is hard or impossible for you to use, contact: GreenlitX — team@greenlitx.com

Please include:

  • The page URL;
  • What you were trying to do;
  • The assistive technology, browser, or device you were using, if you are willing to share it; and
  • How to reach you.

GreenlitX will work on a fix or, where a fix cannot be made promptly, offer an alternative way to get the same essential content or complete the same action — for example, providing project information in text form or accepting a follow, pledge-interest record, or submission through a contact route instead of an inaccessible form.

Accessibility feedback must go to a monitored address. That address must be published in this Section before launch.

6. Changes to this statement

We will update this statement as accessibility work is completed, as known limitations change, and if an independent audit is carried out. Updates will be posted on this page with a revised date.
